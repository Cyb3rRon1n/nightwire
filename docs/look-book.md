# Nightwire Look Book

How our films are shot, in plain English. Every shot in a film should be there for a reason you can say in one line
(the shot's "why"). This book is the menu of reasons.

Our films are short Instagram Reels with a **hopecore** feel: a small warm light in a big dark world. We compose in
landscape and keep everything important inside a tall 9:16 strip in the middle, so it still works on a phone.
Touchstones: *Blade Runner 2049* and *Dune* for vast, beautiful wide shots that make a place feel huge.

## 1. Shot sizes (how much of the person we see)

| Size | Meaning | What it makes you feel | When we use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWS (extreme wide) | the place fills the frame, a person is a speck | awe, loneliness, scale | opening a film, showing how big the city is |
| WS (wide) | whole body, lots of surroundings | belonging to a place, small but safe | our default; establishing and most story beats |
| MWS (medium wide) | knees up | still part of the room, body language readable | walking, sitting down, using a prop |
| MS (medium) | waist up | calm, plain, "just watching" | rare quiet talk, preferably from the side |
| MCU (medium close-up) | chest up | a mood you can read | sparingly, no talking |
| CU (close-up) | the face | intimacy or pressure | a single silent reaction |
| ECU (extreme close-up) | one detail: an eye, a hand | tension, importance | a prop or light, not a face that talks |

We prefer **WS, EWS and MS**. Faces stay small, turned away or in profile.

## 2. Composition (where things sit in the picture)

- **Rule of thirds**: imagine the frame cut into a 3x3 grid; put the subject on a line or crossing, not dead centre. It feels natural and leaves room to breathe.
- **Negative space**: lots of empty frame around the subject. Emptiness reads as scale and loneliness.
- **Figure in the lower third**: a small person at the bottom with sky or city above; the world dwarfs them. Works especially well in 9:16.
- **Leading lines**: roads, rails, rooflines and light strips that point at the subject so the eye travels to them.
- **Frames within frames**: a doorway, window or gap between buildings around the subject. It adds depth and a sense of being watched or sheltered.
- **Silhouettes**: a dark shape against a bright glow. Mood and shape with no face, so nothing for AI to get wrong.
- **Backs and profiles**: we see what they see, or a quiet side view. Invites the viewer in.
- **Reflections**: wet glass, puddles, screens. A second, dreamier image of the same moment.

## 3. Camera

- **Locked-off** (camera fixed on a tripod): steady and patient; our default. The world moves, the camera does not.
- **Slow push-in** (camera drifts closer): attention narrows, feeling builds. Use once per film for the key moment.
- **Slow pan** (camera turns on the spot): reveals a place bit by bit.
- **Why slow?** Fast moves feel anxious and cheap. Slow moves feel confident and heavy, and AI video keeps shapes stable when little changes.
- One move per shot, never a move plus a big character action at the same time.

## 4. Lens feel

- **Wide lens** (small number, e.g. 24 mm): space around the person, depth, loneliness.
- **Long lens** (large number, e.g. 85 mm+): flattens distance and squeezes the background close; feels intimate, like watching from afar.
- Use wide for the big places, long for a quiet figure lit against a glowing wall.

## 5. Light

- **One strong source**: a single sign, window or lamp. Everything else falls into shadow. Clear and moody.
- **Haze** (mist or smoke in the air): makes beams of light visible and softens distance, so places look deep and vast.
- **Sodium amber against cold cyan**: warm orange street light against cool blue-green night. Warm means life and hope; cold means the big dark.
- **Deep blacks**: let shadows be truly dark; the small warm light then glows.

## 6. Time

- **Long holds**: stay on a shot long enough to take it in (4 to 8 seconds).
- **Few cuts**: a Reel of five or six shots beats twenty.
- **Little dialogue**: at most one short line per shot, and often none. Silence plus image is the style.

## 7. Vertical 9:16

We compose wide but keep the subject inside the centre strip. In a tall crop, vastness comes through **height**: tall towers,
big sky, long drops, a tiny figure low in the frame. Put the horizon low or high to exaggerate it.

## 8. Hopecore

A small warm light in a big dark world: one lit window, a lantern, a vending machine, a person kept warm by it. The world
is huge and indifferent, yet there is gentle hope. Frame the warm light small, but make it the brightest thing.

## 9. What the masters do (our own words)

**Blade Runner 2049**
- Huge, still wides with a tiny figure walking through orange haze (the Las Vegas ruins): scale and melancholy through colour and emptiness.
- Monolithic architecture, symmetrical framing, and slow, quiet pacing give a feeling of being small before power.
- Light is often a single, strong, coloured source (rain-lit neon, a bright window) against a very dark room; reflections and glass layer images.

**Dune**
- The desert and ships are shown at gigantic scale, with people as dots against walls of sand or metal: the frame itself is the awe.
- Muted, almost monochrome palettes and lots of empty space make a place feel ancient.
- Backlit silhouettes in haze and dust, and slow, patient movement, carry emotion without close faces.

## 10. Risks: what AI video does badly

Our pre-flight shows these as warnings on the production sheet. They do not block a shot, but plan around them.

- **Talking face at MCU or closer**: lips and identity drift. Use WS/MS, a back, a profile, or let the line be voice-over.
- **More than one line in a shot**: split it across shots.
- **More than two people in frame**: faces and clothes blend. Keep it to two, extras small and far away.
- **Hand actions** (plugging, opening, holding something fiddly): hands warp. Show the result, not the fiddling; or use a silhouette.
- **Camera move plus character action together**: the model loses track. Move the camera or the person, not both.
- **Dialogue-heavy shorts**: more lines than shots. Cut talk, add holds.
